How this is computed
  • Orientation: complementary filter on the raw 100 Hz accel and gyro: the gravity direction is rotated with the gyro (bias = per-axis median) and pulled linearly toward the raw accel with the chosen time constant. Over whole strokes the boat's mean acceleration is about zero, so this converges on true gravity, and surge averages ≈ 0 m/s² at steady speed.
  • Boat frame: up is the session-average gravity direction, forward is the dominant horizontal acceleration, and left = up × forward. Roll and pitch are relative to the average level, with positive roll = starboard side down and positive pitch = bow up. If forward is backwards, everything left/right is mirrored: use Flip forward.
  • Surge / sway / heave: acceleration minus gravity (from the orientation filter), projected on forward / left / up.
  • Strokes: catches are minima of the 4 Hz low-passed surge acceleration that are at least half as deep (adjustable) as the deepest dip within ±2 s, at least 0.7 s apart. This skips the shallow secondary dip each stroke also has. A rhythm check then drops a catch that splits one stroke into two unusually short halves.
  • Pieces in the selection menu are stretches of at least 3 min where the 20 s mean GPS speed stays above 85 % of the session's 90th-percentile speed. Stroke metrics always use the 4 Hz signal; the display filter only affects the charts and overlays.
  • Finish is the release dip: the lowest surge acceleration between the drive peak and 65 % of the stroke. Drive time runs from the catch to the finish. The overlay can align strokes catch → catch or finish → finish, and Shift scrolls the window by a fraction of a stroke.
  • Consistency: the correlation (r) of each stroke's surge curve, resampled to 0–100 %, with the mean curve of the selection, plus the coefficient of variation (CV) of the stroke metrics.
  • Split, distance per stroke and heart rate come from the watch's 1 Hz records (GPS).
